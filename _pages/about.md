Jack Marquez is an HPC Workflows Research Scientist in the Workflows and Ecosystem Services group at Oak Ridge National Laboratory. His work brings AI and high-performance computing together. He designs and studies scientific workflows driven by AI agents that run on leadership-class supercomputers, along with the services and programmatic interfaces that let these agents submit, monitor, and coordinate work on HPC systems. His current research looks at how to provision HPC resources efficiently for agentic workloads, how to make AI-driven decisions reliable and traceable, and how to capture provenance across complex, multi-step scientific campaigns. He works closely with domain scientists in areas such as biology and chemistry, and with collaborators across the DOE national laboratory complex.

Before joining ORNL, he spent four years at the Global Computing Lab at the University of Tennessee. There he did research on workflow systems, HPC reproducibility, and in-situ analytics for molecular dynamics. He received his Ph.D. in Engineering in 2022 from Universidad Autónoma de Occidente in Cali, Colombia, where he had earned his B.Sc. in Informatics Engineering in 2013. He began his academic career there as a researcher in 2013 and became an adjunct professor in 2016.

He has also worked as a software engineer for national and international companies. His research interests include scientific workflows, AI agents for science, high-performance computing, resource management and scheduling, provenance and reproducibility, cloud computing, and performance optimization.
